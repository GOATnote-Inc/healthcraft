\begin{abstract}
HealthCraft is an open environment for evaluating clinical tool-using agents
in synthetic emergency-medicine workflows, adapted from Corecraft. It
combines an in-memory world, a Model Context Protocol tool surface, saved
execution traces, and binary task criteria with an authored safety gate.
The current inventory contains $205$ tasks % CN:post_neg_tasks
and $2{,}323$ criteria. % CN:post_neg_criteria
We distinguish historical benchmark measurements from a subsequent automated
engineering programme focused on preserving evidence and exposing incomplete
execution. Frozen V8 Pass@1 was $24.8\%$ for Claude Opus~4.6 % CN:v8_claude_pass1
and $12.6\%$ for GPT-5.4; % CN:v8_gpt_pass1
these results used a grader subsequently found to fail open and are retained
as superseded measurements, not estimates under the repaired implementation.
The newer engineering work adds source-faithful projections, strict execution
and grading boundaries, immutable attempts, and separately reported completion,
physical storage, readback, and content verification. A source-bound local
development cohort schedules $16$ attempts % CN:local_attempts
across Nemotron Nano and MedGemma on $8$ exposed synthetic reconciliation cases. % CN:local_cases
Of these, $15$ executions completed, % CN:local_completed
$15$ notes were physically stored, % CN:local_stored_notes
and $2$ stored texts were retrieved again; % CN:local_literal_readbacks
none met the full mechanical source-reconciliation contract. % CN:local_mechanical_passed
Clinical criteria were unassessed. The associated engineering checkpoint
records $4{,}588$ passing tests % CN:engineering_tests_passed
and $58$ skipped tests. % CN:engineering_tests_skipped
This is a single-author physician-engineer project using automated development,
verification, and local evaluation. It reports no independent clinical labels,
measured operator benefit, product superiority, or deployment readiness.
Optional offline inspection tools support debugging without introducing a
human-review requirement into execution or publication. The release provides
inspectable negative results and reproducible engineering evidence; prospective
clinical validation and comparative user studies remain future research.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Large language models are already being piloted in clinical decision
support. The evaluation infrastructure that would justify that
deployment has not kept pace. Static medical-QA benchmarks measure
recall of guideline text; they do not measure whether a model will,
under sustained conversational pressure, capitulate and recommend
home care after having correctly recommended emergency transfer; nor
do they measure whether a model that must interact with FHIR
resources over an MCP tool surface will place an anticoagulant order
on a patient with a suspected aortic dissection. These are the
failures that matter in emergency medicine, and static QA cannot see
them.

Reinforcement-learning environments for tool-using agents have begun
to close this gap in other domains. Corecraft
\citep{corecraft2026} introduces a high-fidelity retail/enterprise
environment with an MCP tool surface, a relational world state, a
binary-criteria rubric, and a Docker-bundled training loop.
\textsc{$\tau$-Bench} \citep{tau_bench2024} and BFCL
\citep{bfcl2024} established Pass@k and Pass$^k$ as reliability
metrics for tool use. None of these environments, however, target a
safety-critical clinical setting.

This paper releases an RL environment and benchmark for that setting.
HealthCraft adapts the Corecraft architecture to emergency medicine
with three substantive changes:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The world state contains synthetic clinical entities with
  FHIR-R4-shaped representations and optional OpenEM clinical knowledge.
  A bounded source export has structural/FHIRPath validation evidence;
  complete FHIR conformance and production interoperability remain unestablished.
  \item The reward function is Eq.~1 of Corecraft augmented with a
  hard safety gate: a single unsatisfied safety-critical criterion
  zeros the reward. This is an evaluation convention over authored
  criteria, not a guarantee of clinical safety.
  \item The task suite is grounded in OpenEM's 152 confusion pairs,
  45 decision rules, and 44 evaluation properties, providing
  a documented authoring basis. That provenance does not by itself
  validate every task assertion, clinical inference, or grading rule.
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions are: (a)~a public RL environment for
emergency medicine with a synthetic world state and a 24-tool MCP
surface; (b)~a dual-layer rubric with a hard safety gate, together
with the reward formula (Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}); (c)~a frozen V8 evaluation subset of
195 tasks, 2{,}255 binary criteria, and 515 safety-critical criteria
across six categories; (d)~V8 results on two frontier models,
together with a transparent account of the six infrastructure bugs
whose fix produced the V8 numbers and which invalidate the previously
circulated V7 numbers; and (e)~an independent deterministic overlay
audit (Appendix~\ref{app:v9-overlay}) of the LLM judge over
44 world\_state criteria,  % CN:v9_overlay_criteria
which classifies each disagreement under a six-label taxonomy and
finds that
73\% of disagreements  % CN:v9_judge_halluc_pct
are judge hallucinations rather than overlay faults---a fail-\emph{open}
tendency that a subsequent grader-fidelity audit
(\S\ref{sec:grader-superseded}) confirmed was live on the published grading
path and remediated (fail-closed judge, drug-\emph{class} gates, restored
temporal gates), now guarded by three verdict-locks and a gold-set FP/FN
harness (\S\ref{sec:repro-infra}); and (f)~a separately scoped, automated
source-fidelity development cohort and evidence-inspection workflow
(\S\ref{sec:engineering}). We release the
environment, tasks, rubric, harness, and audit artefacts under
Apache~2.0.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Tool-use RL environments.}
HealthCraft is a direct adaptation of Corecraft \citep{corecraft2026},
which introduces a high-fidelity retail/enterprise RL environment
built around an MCP tool surface over a relational world state, a
binary-criteria rubric, and a Docker-bundled training loop. Our
entity graph, action space, rubric Eq.~1, and Docker topology all
follow Corecraft; the adaptations are (a)~FHIR-R4-shaped clinical entities,
(b)~a hard safety gate on Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}, and (c)~a
domain-specific task suite. \textsc{$\tau$-Bench}
\citep{tau_bench2024} and BFCL \citep{bfcl2024} established the
Pass@k and Pass$^k$ methodology we adopt for tool-use reliability.

\paragraph{Healthcare LLM benchmarks.}
MedQA \citep{medqa2021} evaluates medical question answering, while
HealthBench \citep{healthbench2025} evaluates health-related conversations
with detailed rubrics. HealthCraft adds a simulated stateful tool surface
and execution records. These are different evaluation settings; the work
reported here contains no controlled comparison establishing an advantage
over either benchmark or over commercial evaluation platforms.

\paragraph{Constrained RL and AI safety.}
The hard safety gate corresponds to a constrained-MDP formulation
\citep{altman1999constrained} where the constraint is binary and
zeros reward on violation. \citet{amodei2016concrete} identified
\emph{avoiding negative side effects} and \emph{safe exploration} as
concrete problems; HealthCraft operationalizes both in a clinical
setting. Our safety-critical criteria are the negative side effects
that must not occur; the hard gate is the exploration constraint.

\paragraph{Clinical interoperability.}
FHIR R4 \citep{fhir_r4} informs the environment's clinical entity
representations and bundle-generation helpers. Runtime entities are
Python dataclasses and dictionaries. A separate frozen roster-source
export was checked with the official HL7 validator, with warnings and
terminology limitations retained (\S\ref{sec:engineering}). That bounded
result neither validates the entire world nor demonstrates interoperability
with a production EHR.

\section{Environment Design}
\label{sec:env}
% ============================================================

HealthCraft's Docker scaffold provisions three services: PostgreSQL,
a tool server, and a task engine (\texttt{docker/}). The running tool
server uses an in-memory \texttt{WorldState}; no runtime code connects
it to the provisioned PostgreSQL service. Session mutations are retained
in that process, and trajectories are saved separately as JSON artifacts
(Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). The container topology follows Corecraft's
Table~2, but the bundle alone does not run Megatron, SGLang, or GRPO.
The Python RL interfaces provide the environment and training reward;
an external trainer owns optimization and weight synchronization.

\subsection{State space}
\label{sec:state}
The world state consists of in-memory entity collections populated by a
deterministic seed (seed=42)  % CN:seed
covering 14 entity types  % CN:entity_types
and 4{,}075 entities in the base installation.  % CN:entities_seeded
With the OpenEM source corpus available, the same seed produces
4{,}450 entities; the optional knowledge corpus also changes generated
clinical-task counts.  % CN:entities_seeded_openem
Patients, encounters, beds, staff, clinical tasks, treatment plans,
protocols, decision rules, conditions, medications, supplies,
insurance, and reference materials are synthetic records; the release
does not establish formal FHIR R4 conformance for these collections.
Figure~\ref{fig:entity-graph} shows the conceptual reference structure
of the graph, including transfer records from the original design that
are not seeded as a standalone collection. Entity invariants
(e.g.\ every \textsc{Encounter.patient\_id} resolves to a live
\textsc{Patient}; bed occupancy is conserved) are covered by repository
tests rather than a formal FHIR validation pass. The base installation includes bundled
clinical knowledge; an optional OpenEM source checkout supplies
370 conditions,  % CN:openem_conditions
in addition to the confusion pairs, decision rules, and evaluation
properties used in task authorship (\S\ref{sec:tasks}).

\subsection{Action space}
\label{sec:actions}
The agent interacts with the world through 24 MCP tools  % CN:n_tools
organized into four waves  % CN:tool_waves
by capability. Wave~1 (read-only) consists of the twelve
search/get tools for patients, encounters, clinical knowledge,
reference materials, available resources, conditions, patient history,
protocols, transfer status, and insurance. Wave~2 (computation) has
four tools: \texttt{checkResourceAvailability},
\texttt{calculateTransferTime}, \texttt{runDecisionRule}, and
\texttt{validateTreatmentPlan}. Wave~3 (mutation) has six tools:
\texttt{createClinicalOrder}, \texttt{updateTaskStatus},
\texttt{updateEncounter}, \texttt{updatePatientRecord},
\texttt{registerPatient}, and \texttt{applyProtocol}. Wave~4
(workflows) has two composite operations: \texttt{processDischarge}
and \texttt{processTransfer}. The full tool schema---arguments,
return types, and error codes---appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:tools}; the canonical JSON Schema lives in
\texttt{configs/mcp-tools.json}.

Tool names follow Corecraft's camelCase MCP convention at the server
boundary and are mapped to snake\_case Python handlers internally. The
sole addition relative to Corecraft's 23-tool set is
\texttt{runDecisionRule}, which returns the output of a named clinical
decision rule (e.g.\ PERC, Wells, HEART) given a structured input. The
other 23 tools are direct analogues of Corecraft's retail/enterprise
tools.

\subsection{Tool semantics and error model}
\label{sec:semantics}
In the V8 evaluation configuration, tool calls are deterministic given
the world state: no latency is
simulated, no retry or timeout semantics are modelled, and no
partial-success or idempotency markers are attached to the mutating
operations. Every call returns either
$\{\texttt{status}{:}\,\texttt{"ok"},\ \texttt{data}{:}\,\ldots\}$
or
$\{\texttt{status}{:}\,\texttt{"error"},\ \texttt{code}{:}\,\ldots,\ \texttt{message}{:}\,\ldots\}$
and is recorded in an append-only audit log that the world state
exposes to the rubric grader. The current RL scaffold additionally
provides optional seeded latency and transient-failure injection;
mutation idempotency is enabled by default. These additions do not
retroactively change the historical V8 configuration or its saved results.

Per Corecraft \S5.5, HealthCraft injects six classes of
structured noise that are seeded deterministically so that evaluations
remain reproducible: (i)~pagination limits (search tools cap at $10$
results with no \texttt{hasMore} flag, exposing the ``failure to
paginate'' pattern from Corecraft \S4.1); (ii)~occasional clock skew
between triage and nursing notes; (iii)~incomplete records (some
patients lack insurance; some encounters lack disposition); (iv)~stale
data (lab results delayed by 2 hours, vitals not yet posted);
(v)~ambiguous documentation (abbreviations, unclear prior notes);
(vi)~red-herring abnormal findings that are irrelevant to the presenting
problem. Noise is part of the environment specification, not of the
task authorship.

\subsection{FHIR integration and OpenEM bridge}
\label{sec:fhir}
The live tool runtime stores synthetic entity objects in
\texttt{src/healthcraft/world/state.py}.
\texttt{src/healthcraft/world/fhir\_store.py} provides a separate
dictionary-backed resource store, and
\texttt{src/healthcraft/openem/fhir\_adapter.py} provides synthetic
patient and encounter bundle helpers. Neither supplies a PostgreSQL
connection or formal FHIR profile validation. At seed time,
\texttt{src/healthcraft/world/seed.py} optionally loads OpenEM condition
frontmatter; when the corpus is unavailable, it uses the bundled
clinical-knowledge fallback. This optional corpus path is distinct from
task authorship using OpenEM metadata and from validated EHR integration.

\subsection{Determinism}
\label{sec:determinism}
Entity generation and noise injection use a configured
seed (seed=42)  % CN:seed
in \texttt{src/healthcraft/world/seed.py}. Repeated seeding preserves
entity identities and seeded attributes, but some creation/update timestamps
and clinical-task due-times still use the wall clock. Fresh worlds are
therefore not byte-identical; saved source snapshots and execution traces
are needed to bind a reproduction to its actual inputs. Model \emph{sampling}
is a separate matter:
the V8 models (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4) ran at temperature=0,  % CN:temperature
but neither current frontier model supports it---Opus 4.7+ deprecated
the parameter (the API returns 400) and gpt-5.5-class reasoning models
reject \texttt{temperature=0} ("only the default value is supported").
The orchestrator self-heals (it omits the parameter for those models),
which means their sampling falls back to the provider default and is
\emph{not} reproducible run-to-run. We also send no provider-side
sampling seed. Reproducibility for such models therefore rests on the
recorded environment configuration plus multi-trial Pass@$k$ with confidence intervals,
not on bit-identical trajectories; the $\text{Pass@3} > \text{Pass@1}$
gap in \S\ref{sec:results} reflects observed run variation. These measurements
do not separate model sampling variation from provider-side nondeterminism.

\section{Reward Function}
\label{sec:reward}
% ============================================================
% Phase-2 anchor: the reward formula is fixed and derived from
% src/healthcraft/tasks/rubrics.py:compute_reward (lines 55-86). Prose
% discussion and verification-method breakdown added in Phase 3.

Let $\tau$ denote a trajectory, $C$ the set of binary criteria for the
task, $C_{\text{safety}} \subseteq C$ the subset flagged
\texttt{safety\_critical}, and $\operatorname{sat}(c,\tau) \in \{0,1\}$
the indicator that criterion $c$ is satisfied by $\tau$. Evaluation reward is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reward}
r(\tau) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } \exists\, c \in C_{\text{safety}} : \operatorname{sat}(c, \tau) = 0, \\[2pt]
\dfrac{1}{|C|} \displaystyle\sum_{c \in C} \operatorname{sat}(c, \tau) & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and a trajectory is counted as \emph{passed} iff every criterion is
satisfied:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pass}
\operatorname{Pass}(\tau) \;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[\,\forall c \in C:\ \operatorname{sat}(c, \tau) = 1\,\right].
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:reward} is Corecraft's Eq.~1 augmented with a hard
safety gate: a single unsatisfied safety-critical criterion zeros the
reward regardless of how many other criteria are satisfied. The gate is
the central design choice. Emergency medicine tolerates no tradeoff
between a lethal error and an otherwise competent performance, so the
reward surface must reflect that. This makes the landscape non-convex
in a way that Corecraft's retail domain does not require: our models
routinely satisfy $10$ of $11$ criteria on a task and score $0$ because
the $11$th was safety-critical (\S\ref{sec:results}). The
\texttt{compute\_reward} function in
\texttt{src/healthcraft/tasks/rubrics.py}
is the source of truth for Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}. The separate experimental
training reward in \texttt{src/healthcraft/rl/reward.py} uses verifiable
anchoring, restraint folding, and judge abstention; it is not Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}.

\paragraph{Verification methods.}
Each criterion declares how it is to be verified. The three methods
are:
(i)~\emph{world\_state}, a deterministic check against the append-only
audit log emitted by the MCP layer; a criterion such as ``the agent did
not order anticoagulation'' resolves to a predicate over the audit
entries and is evaluated without an LLM in the loop;
(ii)~\emph{llm\_judge}, a cross-vendor judge (\S\ref{sec:eval}) that
reads the full trajectory and returns a binary decision with evidence;
(iii)~\emph{pattern}, a regex match over concatenated assistant-turn text,
retained for light-weight presentational checks (e.g.\ discharge
instructions contain return-precautions language).
We prefer \emph{world\_state} wherever a deterministic predicate is
expressible. Prior work on \ifanon a radiology evaluation harness\else
the RadSlice harness\fi{} motivates this preference: L0 pattern
checks alone had a 75\% false-positive rate; combining them with
state verification eliminated that mode. For HealthCraft, this
choice is particularly consequential because many safety-critical
criteria concern \emph{whether an order was placed}, which is a
well-defined query over the audit log.

\paragraph{Dimensions are diagnostic, not reward.}
The rubric also labels each criterion with one of six weighted
dimensions: \textsf{clinical\_completeness} ($0.20$),
\textsf{clinical\_correctness} ($0.25$),
\textsf{protocol\_adherence} ($0.15$),
\textsf{documentation\_quality} ($0.10$),
\textsf{safety} ($0.20$), and
\textsf{temporal\_sequencing} ($0.10$). These weights do not enter
Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}. They are an analytical lens used only for error
decomposition in post-hoc analysis: when a model fails a task, we want
to know \emph{why}, and grouping criteria by dimension surfaces
patterns (e.g.\ ``the model satisfied completeness but failed
temporal\_sequencing on $80\%$ of multi-step tasks''). Conflating the
weights with the reward would re-introduce the very tradeoff the
safety gate was introduced to prevent.

\section{Task Suite}
\label{sec:tasks}
% ============================================================

The public benchmark consists of
195 tasks  % CN:n_tasks
distributed across six categories,  % CN:n_categories
graded against
2{,}255 binary criteria  % CN:n_criteria
of which
515 are safety-critical.  % CN:n_safety_critical
Each criterion is a single proposition with a single verification
method; the binary form is load-bearing because it composes cleanly
under Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}. Table~\ref{tab:tasks} summarizes the
distribution; appendix~\ref{app:criteria} decomposes criteria by
dimension.

Tasks carry a difficulty level from $1$ (\emph{Triage}, 1--2 expected
tool calls) to $5$ (\emph{Mass Casualty}, 15+ coordinated calls across
multiple encounters). Difficulty is not weighted into the reward; it
is used for per-level analysis and for sampling during training.

\paragraph{Generation sources.}
Clinical-reasoning tasks are scaffolded from OpenEM's 152 confusion
pairs  % CN:n_confusion_pairs
---pairs of conditions whose presentations overlap but whose
management diverges (e.g.\ STEMI vs.\ aortic dissection). The task
asks the agent to disambiguate given FHIR evidence, and the
safety-critical criterion is typically a negated action
(``did not order anticoagulation'' when dissection is in the
differential). Information-retrieval tasks are scaffolded from
OpenEM's 45 decision rules.  % CN:n_decision_rules
Safety-critical-judgment tasks draw from OpenEM's 44 evaluation
properties  % CN:n_eval_properties
together with EMTALA and capacity scenarios authored in-house.
Multi-step workflows, clinical communication, and temporal reasoning
are authored categories; each follows a coverage-cycle methodology
adapted from \ifanon a prior emergency-medicine evaluation harness\else
LostBench\fi{} in which gaps are identified by scoring the current
corpus against a target distribution and filling the largest gaps
first.

\paragraph{Release contents.}
Each task ships as a YAML file specifying the description, world
setting (seed, timestamp, active encounter IDs), criteria list, and
metadata. The full task corpus is published alongside the
environment under Apache~2.0.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval}
% ============================================================

An evaluation run consists of: (a)~for each of 195 tasks, seed a
fresh in-memory world for each trial using the configured deterministic
seed schedule; (b)~drive the agent for up to $N$ turns (category-dependent)
against the MCP server; (c)~capture the complete audit log and
assistant/tool message trail; (d)~apply the rubric grader to each
trajectory; (e)~aggregate per-model, per-category, and per-task
statistics. Each $(\text{model},\text{task})$ pair is run for
3 trials,  % CN:v8_trials
yielding
585 runs per model  % CN:v8_total_runs
across the
195 tasks.  % CN:v8_total_tasks
The orchestrator (\texttt{src/healthcraft/llm/orchestrator.py},
function \texttt{run\_frontier\_evaluation}) is idempotent: it
resumes from existing trajectory files on disk, so partial runs can
be continued without re-billing the model.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Following \textsc{$\tau$-Bench} \citep{tau_bench2024}\ifanon\else{} and
LostBench\fi, we report
\textsc{Pass@1} (mean pass rate across all trials),
\textsc{Pass@3} (at least one of three trials passes, best-case
reliability),
\textsc{Pass$^3$} (all three trials pass, worst-case reliability), the
expected reward $\mathbb{E}[r(\tau)]$ under Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}, and
the safety-failure rate (fraction of trials with at least one
unsatisfied safety-critical criterion). Wilson score 95\% confidence
intervals are reported for all proportions; the derivation is in
Appendix~\ref{app:wilson}.

\paragraph{Cross-vendor judging.}
Judge selection forbids self-judging: Claude is judged by GPT,
GPT is judged by Claude, and Gemini is judged by Claude
(pinned in \texttt{judge.py}). Temperature was held at $0$ for both agent
and judge in the V8 run; current frontier models no longer support that
setting (\S\ref{sec:determinism}). The judge sees the full trajectory and
returns a JSON-structured verdict per criterion. Judge agreement was
quantified on a 100-trajectory dual-graded sample:
Cohen's $\kappa=0.553$,  % CN:judge_kappa
moderate agreement by Landis--Koch---measured, however, under the v1 judge

\subsection{Methodological transparency: superseded V7}
\label{sec:v7-superseded}

Between pilots v2 and v8 we fixed
six infrastructure bugs  % CN:infra_bugs_fixed
in the harness---none of them in the agents, the judge, or the
rubric, and none of them known at the time of the v7 results that
we had previously circulated. The six bugs were:
(i)~the evaluator silently dropped non-terminal qualifiers when
parsing tool arguments, preventing some safety-critical criteria from
ever being satisfiable;
(ii)~tool-schema mismatches between the server-side JSON Schema and
the agent-visible MCP schema caused confident tool calls to fail
validation on previously valid input;
(iii)~entity-injection gaps left some FHIR bundles missing
\textsc{ServiceRequest} links that certain tasks' criteria required;
(iv)~system-prompt composition double-appended policy blocks in
certain task variants, pushing relevant clinical context out of the
token budget;
(v)~OpenEM loading had a fallback path that silently degraded to five
bundled conditions when the full corpus was unavailable; and
(vi)~\texttt{processTransfer} had an ordering bug that marked a
transfer complete before the receiving-facility handshake was
recorded in the audit log. V8 resolves all six; the corresponding
PR chain is logged in \texttt{docs/V8\_ANALYSIS.md} and
\texttt{docs/EVALUATION\_INTEGRITY.md}.

Offline re-scoring of V7 trajectories under the V8 evaluator produced
611 criterion-level verdict flips overall (602 satisfied-to-unsatisfied
from the qualifier-enforcement fix; 9 unsatisfied-to-satisfied from
the compound-clause fix) with zero additional flips under the V8
evaluator applied to V8 trajectories, which we took as a lower bound
on evaluator stability. We now retract that reading
(\S\ref{sec:grader-superseded}): ``zero additional flips'' was an
artefact of testing the V8 evaluator against its own prior verdicts;
re-applying the \emph{corrected} grader flips 12 of 88 channel-locked
verdicts. The three tool-side bugs
(\texttt{processTransfer} $\times 2$ plus the missing
entity-ID injection) primarily deflated GPT-5.4 because GPT follows
schema parameter names literally while Claude adapts to error
messages; this asymmetry is the mechanism behind the qualitative
re-ordering reported below. We did not run a controlled ablation of
each bug in isolation because the six fixes were landed as one PR
chain against a single rollout harness; re-instrumenting v7 to
toggle bugs individually was judged more likely to introduce new
artefacts than to cleanly decompose the observed delta.

The pilot deltas are instructive. On average reward, Claude Opus~4.6
\emph{drops} from v7 to v8 by about $13\%$ relative  % CN:v7_to_v8_claude_delta
(0.730$\to$0.634) while GPT-5.4 \emph{rises} by about $107\%$ relative  % CN:v7_to_v8_gpt_delta
(0.264$\to$0.546). Pass@1 follows the same directions with smaller
magnitudes (Claude 26.8\%$\to$24.8\%, GPT 4.6\%$\to$12.6\%). We are
cautious about assigning causation---the six bug fixes were committed
in a single chain and their isolated effects were not ablated---but the
direction of the flip is consistent across metrics. We highlight the
transparency here because the alternative (treating the v7 numbers as
authoritative, or burying the correction in a footnote) would have
meant publishing a result whose headline ordering is at least partly
an artefact of harness bugs. The broader lesson is that for tool-use
benchmarks, infrastructure fidelity is not a separate axis from model
evaluation; it is part of what is being measured.

\subsection{Methodological transparency: superseded grader}
\label{sec:grader-superseded}

The V7 correction above fixed the harness. After it, we turned the same
adversarial scrutiny on the \emph{grader} and found that infrastructure
fidelity extends to the scoring path itself. A multi-agent grader-fidelity
audit (documented in \texttt{docs/GRADER\_FIDELITY\_AUDIT\_2026-05-31.md},
PR chain \#14--\#24) found defects on the live grading path that produced
the V8/V9 numbers:
(i)~the LLM judge's keyword fallback failed \emph{open}---on unparseable or
truncated JSON it returned \texttt{satisfied=True}, including on
safety-critical criteria, with one contaminated verdict found live in a V9
trajectory; \texttt{compute\_reward} and the safety gate likewise treated a
missing safety result as ``safe'';
(ii)~drug gates matched a single literal token rather than the contraindicated
\emph{class}, so a sibling agent (e.g.\ a non-heparin anticoagulant) slipped a
``did not administer'' gate;
(iii)~a bare tool-name check with empty parameters collapsed many
``ordered $X$'' safety criteria onto \emph{any} single order;
(iv)~six time-critical safety gates had been flattened into existence checks
blind to ordering (a lethal false-\textsc{pass} shape), now reverted to
judge grading; and
(v)~substring over-match and compound-clause handling produced false
\textsc{fail}s on correct trajectories.
We fixed each (judge now fails \emph{closed}, built at a negation-first prompt
version; drug \emph{classes}; both terms required for compound criteria;
temporal gates restored), and a structural check-linter blocks the
recurrence of the malformed-check patterns.

The direction matters for a safety benchmark: a judge that fails open on a
\emph{negated} safety gate \emph{deflates} the measured safety-failure rate,
so the V8/V9 safety-failure numbers are biased \emph{low}---the defect made
models look safer than they were. Per operator decision we do not re-grade
the cached V8/V9 trajectories for this revision; their aggregates are
therefore \textbf{superseded, not re-measured}, and should be read as
pre-audit. As a regression check of the \emph{corrected} grader we report a
curated set of 55 cases run through the real graders: \textbf{zero}
false-\textsc{pass}, \textbf{zero} false-\textsc{fail}, and \textbf{zero}
safety-critical false-\textsc{pass}. The roughly 20\% Wilson upper bound
is for the v10 world-state false-pass stratum's $n=15$ true-violation % CN:goldset_v10_negative_cases
cases, not all $55$ cases. Reviewer identities and an adjudication protocol
are not included in the released case artifact. This measures grader
\emph{mechanics} on curated cases, not a population clinical-error rate or the judge's
clinical agreement with physicians, which remains future work
(\S\ref{sec:limits}). As with V7, we did not ablate each grader fix in
isolation. We highlight this because a safety benchmark whose grader silently
certified unsafe behavior as safe must say so plainly rather than reprint the
affected numbers as clean.

\subsection{Reproducibility infrastructure}
\label{sec:repro-infra}

The audit's durable output is tamper-evidence the v1 release lacked. Three
regression locks now pin grading behavior: a golden-trajectory replay lock
(30 stratified V8 trajectories, byte-identical reward/pass/safety-gate);
a channel verdict-lock over 88 trajectories (64 of 64 overlay-promoted tasks
covered) that pins the criteria-level verdict hash at each rubric channel, so
any silent shift in an overlay, the vocabulary, or the matcher turns CI red
(12 of 88 v10 verdicts already differ from V8, behavior the V8 lock could not
see); and the structural check-linter. These guard \emph{reproducibility and
non-drift}, which is complementary to---not a substitute for---correctness: a
locked verdict that is wrong stays wrong until additional evidence or
review exposes it. Historical metrics retain their recorded rubric channel;
the post-audit frontier comparison uses the \texttt{v10} channel (the
strictest populated, regression-locked channel); a \texttt{v11} consensus
channel exists but is empty by design and its proposer is unaudited, so we do
not rely on it.

\section{Automated Engineering Evidence}
\label{sec:engineering}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Method and separation of evidence}
The current project is conducted by one physician-engineer author with
AI-assisted development and automated tests, execution capture, verification,
and report generation. Synthetic scenarios and their expected source ledgers
share the project's authoring process. Separately implemented verifiers and
agent-based development reviews are engineering checks, not independent
physician adjudication. The automated workflow can run and publish without a
human-in-the-loop step. External clinical review is a possible future study,
not a completed result or a condition of this software release.

Historical benchmark trajectories are immutable. New task profiles, local
model cohorts, and diagnostic reports carry their own source and configuration
identities; they do not silently replace earlier scores. The development
cohort below has no clinical benchmark score and does not modify the primary
evaluation reward in Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}. Its mechanical verification asks
whether recorded source facts and identities survived retrieval and storage,
not whether those source assertions constitute medical truth.

\subsection{Source fidelity and execution integrity}
The current native projections retain authored care and imaging assertions,
raw source fields, explicit temporal precision, and missing information.
An order, a reported administration, and a pending study remain distinct;
a documentation timestamp is not silently promoted to a measurement time.
Linked-history and roster profiles expose selected task facts through real
tools without manufacturing absent clinical observations. Their witnesses
establish bounded task reachability and source conservation, not that every
historical task is clinically valid or solvable.

Execution now preserves partial traces on provider and tool failures,
validates finite JSON before dispatch, and distinguishes a failed response
from a known world-state outcome. Retry keys bind the request, while changed
requests conflict instead of silently reusing a previous mutation. Resumption
retains earlier attempts. Assessment entry points reject empty rubrics;
standalone grading refuses to replace existing outputs. The explicit
simulation entry point produces unassessed smoke evidence rather than
model-labelled benchmark scores. Reports keep errors, unknown completion,
ungraded criteria, and recorded verdicts separate. These are software
contracts demonstrated by regression tests, not clinical outcome findings.

Semantic task validity remains incomplete. Documented replay counterexamples
show that IR-001-C03 credits a bed/resource lookup for a drug cross-reactivity
assertion, that IR-002 tool-name checks can credit another patient's or empty
history, and that SCJ-012-C02's historical overlay accepts an unrelated
medication order for an antibiotic-and-timing assertion. The historical task
contracts remain unchanged; the isolated linked-history profile is a new
engineering experiment, not a correction to old scores. These cases, recorded
in \texttt{docs/TASK\_VALIDITY\_FINDINGS.md}, show why a passing curated grader
regression set does not establish rubric-wide semantic validity. Task-authoring
metadata also retains schema mismatches; syntactic reachability and execution
tests are not claims of full task-schema conformance.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limits}
% ============================================================

We describe the real limits of this release directly. One of them---the
grader-fidelity defects in \S\ref{sec:grader-superseded}, including an
LLM-judge that failed \emph{open} on safety-critical criteria---does bear
on the trustworthiness of the V8/V9 numbers reported above, which we
therefore present as produced under a now-superseded grader (not yet
re-measured). The remainder constrain what the results can be used to
claim.

\paragraph{Scope and non-claims.}
HealthCraft measures \emph{execution correctness} under a fixed
rubric in a seeded synthetic clinical tool-use environment; it does not
measure clinical reasoning under uncertainty, calibrated risk
judgement, or deployment-time behaviour. Safety is operationalized as
binary criterion satisfaction with a hard gate, not as dynamic risk
tradeoff management. Seeded generation controls selected environmental
variation but does not eliminate wall-clock metadata or due-time differences
(\S\ref{sec:determinism}). Stored inputs are necessary for exact replay;
seed equality alone does not establish it. Model
\emph{sampling} is not deterministic for current frontier models: Opus 4.7+
and gpt-5.5 no longer support \texttt{temperature=0}
(\S\ref{sec:determinism}), so their runs reproduce only up to the fixed
environment plus multi-trial Pass@$k$, not bit-identical trajectories.
FHIR-R4-shaped representations describe a data-design choice; verified
interoperability, team communication, interruptions, and the social
dynamics of an emergency department remain outside the evidence. A
strong result on HealthCraft does not establish clinical readiness;
the relationship between benchmark scores and clinical performance
has not been prospectively validated.

\paragraph{Static patient state in the historical evaluation.}
In the V8 configuration, patient state does not evolve between agent actions. Vitals do not
trend, the septic patient does not decompensate, and the triaged-low
patient does not bounce back to triage. Emergency medicine is a
temporal discipline, and this is the largest single fidelity gap in
the evaluated environment. The current code includes optional seeded
vitals trajectories and simplified action-conditioned physiology in the
RL path. Their clinical fidelity and effect on benchmark outcomes have
not been established by the historical results reported here.

\paragraph{Tool timing in the historical evaluation.}
The historical evaluation uses instantaneous, deterministic tool calls. Real MCP
endpoints fail, time out, and return partial results; real clinical
systems have queueing and contention. The current RL scaffold supplies
optional seeded simulated latency, transient errors, and retry budgets
through \texttt{src/healthcraft/mcp/faults.py}; it is not a full
concurrency or clinical queueing model. No empirical robustness gain
from these additions is established by the reported evaluations.

\paragraph{Idempotency in the historical tool surface.}
The V8 wave~3 tool surface did not accept an idempotency key, so a
retried \texttt{createClinicalOrder} could create a duplicate order.
The historical tasks did not exercise this retry behavior. The current
runtime supports idempotency keys with duplicate-mutation protection
enabled by default; the V8 evaluation described here predates that
implementation and does not measure its effect.

\paragraph{LLM-judge reliability.}
Cohen's $\kappa = 0.553$ is moderate, not substantial, and our
oracle-validation bridge (\S\ref{sec:eval}) covers only the
\texttt{world\_state}-verifiable subset of criteria. For criteria
graded by \texttt{llm\_judge}, there is real residual variance that
our intervals do not capture. Stratified by trajectory length
(appendix~\ref{app:judge}), $\kappa$ is lowest in the long-trajectory
regime ($>40$ turns, $\kappa=0.306$) where raw agreement is in fact
highest ($92.0\%$); this base-rate paradox matters for Claude
(median $\sim 49$ turns) more than for GPT-5.4 (median $\sim 8$). The
deterministic overlay audit (Appendix~\ref{app:v9-overlay}) sharpens
this: of 63 disagreements between the overlay and the V8 judge,
46 are judge hallucinations  % CN:v9_judge_halluc_count
(73\% of disagreements), and 5 of the 6 safety-critical
inversions  % CN:v9_safety_inversions
are judge PASS verdicts on trajectories in which the audit log
contains no successful qualifying tool call---i.e.\ the judge
invented the passing behaviour. For the Tier~1 subset of 10 overlay
entries with no disagreements in the audited trials,  % CN:v9_tier1_count
we recommend replacing \texttt{llm\_judge} with
the deterministic rule for reward; Tier~2 (26 entries,  % CN:v9_tier2_count
research-only) and Tier~3 (8 entries,  % CN:v9_tier3_count
keep \texttt{llm\_judge}) are recommendations to retain the LLM grader
pending further review. These tiers are analytical recommendations:
the runtime loader applies all configured channel entries without a
tier filter. The partial mitigation
is to prefer \texttt{world\_state} at authoring time and to apply
Tier~1 overlays where available. Further automated counterexamples and
source-bound checks can improve engineering validity. An independent clinical
adjudication study could assess agreement with physicians; none is reported
here, and no such study is required to run or publish this engineering workflow.
Most consequentially, the 2026-05-31 grader-fidelity audit
(\S\ref{sec:grader-superseded}) found the production-default judge failed
\emph{open}---on unparseable or truncated output it returned
\texttt{satisfied=True}, including on safety-critical criteria. The
five-of-six safety inversions above are a symptom of this, not residual
variance. The judge is now built at a negation-first prompt version that
fails \emph{closed}; \texttt{judge\_model} and \texttt{judge\_prompt\_version}
are persisted per trajectory; and a curated 55-case regression set finds
zero safety-critical false-\textsc{pass}. The $\sim 20\%$ Wilson upper bound
belongs to the v10 world-state false-pass stratum with $n=15$ true-violation
cases, not to all $55$ cases. Reviewer identities and an adjudication
protocol are not included in the released case artifact. This measures
grader \emph{mechanics} on curated cases, not a population clinical-error
rate; clinical agreement with physicians remains the open item.

\paragraph{Residual grader gaps and claim limits.}
Three residuals we state plainly. (i)~The corrected drug gates match curated
\texttt{em\_vocab} \emph{classes}, but those classes are hand-maintained
allowlists with known omissions (\texttt{thrombolytic} lacks
reteplase/streptokinase; \texttt{anticoagulant} lacks dalteparin/tinzaparin),
so the member-not-class failure mode is \emph{narrowed, not eliminated}---an
external drug ontology (RxNorm/ATC) is the durable fix. (ii)~The neutral judge
used for the frontier run (grok-4) has \emph{unmeasured} clinical agreement
(no $\kappa$). Fail-closed parsing assigns a failed criterion to an
unparseable response, preventing that parser failure from creating a pass.
This does not establish the direction of aggregate grading error: semantic
false passes and false failures remain possible, and their combined magnitude
and direction are unknown.
(iii)~No deployment-readiness claim follows from any HealthCraft result: a
held-out, prospective clinical validation has not been performed, and
a model trained against this reward is a research artifact behind a
\texttt{ResearchArtifactMetadata} firewall. This lack limits the claims; it
does not block publication of the automated engineering evidence.

\paragraph{Infrastructure-bug isolation.}
The V7-to-V8 delta (\S\ref{sec:v7-superseded}) is reported as a bundle
of six simultaneous fixes. We did not ablate individual bugs; the
qualitative re-ordering of Claude and GPT-5.4 is therefore a
\emph{joint} effect of the six, not attributable to any single one.
Per-bug direction of impact is recorded in
\texttt{docs/EVALUATION\_INTEGRITY.md}. Independent replication of v7
under the v8 evaluator would be the natural next step for
external validation of the fixed numbers. A \emph{second} wave of
defects---in the grader rather than the harness---was found after V8
(\S\ref{sec:grader-superseded}), so the six-fix narrative is not terminal:
infrastructure fidelity extends to the scoring path itself.

\paragraph{Task coverage.}
The 195 tasks sample from OpenEM's 370 conditions at an average of
under one task per condition (the live corpus is 205 tasks / 2{,}323
criteria / 529 safety-critical after the negative-class slate; the
195/2{,}255/515 figures are the frozen V8 evaluation subset). There are
known coverage gaps
(paediatric subspecialties, some toxicology presentations, most
obstetric emergencies); the coverage-cycle methodology makes them
visible but does not close them in this release.

\paragraph{Task-distribution balance and the negative-class slate.}
The V8 slate over-samples action-positive scenarios where
the correct behaviour is to order, escalate, or admit and
under-samples action-negative ones where the correct
behaviour is restraint. As a shipped structural fix we
committed a $10$-task % CN:neg_tasks
negative-class slate (NEG-001 through NEG-010,
Appendix~\ref{app:v9-overlay}). Its current inventory contains $68$ criteria, % CN:neg_criteria
of which $14$ are safety-critical. % CN:neg_safety_critical
The current live inventory contains $205$ tasks % CN:post_neg_tasks
and $2{,}323$ criteria, measured independently of the frozen V8 counts. % CN:post_neg_criteria
A 60-run smoke pilot (\texttt{docs/NEG\_SMOKE\_PILOT.md})
establishes that the slate rebalances \emph{task-level}
difficulty: mean Pass@1 is $0.500$ % CN:neg_smoke_claude_pass1
for Claude Opus 4.7 and $0.533$ % CN:neg_smoke_gpt_pass1
for GPT-5.4, versus V8 corpus means of $0.248$ and $0.126$.
It also caught two iatrogenic patterns the V8 corpus does
not surface: insulin infusion on alcoholic ketoacidosis
($4/6$ trials across both models) % CN:neg_smoke_aka_insulin
and tPA on hypoglycaemic aphasia
($2/6$ trials). % CN:neg_smoke_tpa_hypoglycemic
The pilot also \emph{falsifies} the Appendix F.5 projection
that NEG criteria would average ${\sim}0.65$ criterion-PASS
and pull audit-subset prevalence into the target band:
observed NEG-criterion prevalence is $0.929$. % CN:neg_smoke_crit_prev
Restraint criteria are trivially satisfied whenever the
agent has been trained against the specific over-action the
criterion forbids, so the kappa-paradox mitigation that
requires additional evidence about reference-label validity and task
prevalence (Appendix~\ref{app:v9-overlay}); independent clinical adjudication
is one possible future study, not an ongoing or required release step.

\paragraph{Evaluation-to-training boundary.}
Equation~\ref{eq:reward} remains the evaluation reward. The implemented
RL path instead reads \texttt{compute\_training\_reward} in
\texttt{src/healthcraft/rl/reward.py}: a separate experimental objective
that gates on safety and restraint, combines verifiable and judged
criteria, and supports a capped process term. The NEG smoke-pilot's
overall criterion-PASS prevalence of $0.929$ % CN:neg_smoke_crit_prev
motivates this design; it is not a measurement of training outcomes or
proof that this alternative prevents reward hacking. The coupling to
Megatron+SGLang+GRPO follows Corecraft~\S5.2, but optimization remains
the responsibility of an external trainer. We do not claim
training-safety; closing this
boundary requires a soft-gate/hard-gate ablation, restraint-criterion
reweighting, and reward-hacking probes that we leave as future work.

\paragraph{Underpowered for scaling-law claims.}
The historical two-model main results are diagnostic but
underpowered for scaling-law claims. Corecraft Table~1 reports six
frontier configurations; we cite it where broader context is needed
and defer our own scaling analysis.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Clinical validation of the optional dynamic-state model; live external
RL training runs (Megatron + SGLang + GRPO per Corecraft \S5.2);
training-reward ablations; task-set scaling to a
$\sim\!1000$-task training split kept disjoint from the
evaluation split; optional independent clinical assessment of HealthCraft
criteria; comparative developer/operator studies; further controlled evaluation on the
post-NEG $205$-task corpus.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

HealthCraft provides a synthetic clinical tool-use environment and an
automated engineering workflow for inspecting agent execution. Its current
contribution is measurable evidence preservation: source assertions remain
attributable, interrupted attempts remain visible, and physical actions are
reported separately from strict content verdicts. The primary rubric remains
an authored research measure, not a clinical-safety certificate.

Historical benchmark results are preserved under their original grading
conditions, including the documented defects. The newer local development
cohort demonstrates why successful termination and a persisted note do not
establish faithful reconciliation. It does not establish clinical performance,
reliable model ordering, operator benefit or superiority to competing systems.

The single-author workflow publishes source, tests, immutable traces and
limitations without requiring a human approval stage. Optional review tools
provide additional inspection paths; prospective clinical and comparative
user validation remain research opportunities. The environment, task suite
and evaluation code are available under Apache~2.0
\ifanon at a URL redacted for blind review\else at
\url{https://github.com/GOATnote-Inc/healthcraft}\fi;
third-party integrations retain their own notices. The external RL coupling
and experimental training reward require separate empirical evaluation.
