Abstract:
HealthCraft is an open environment for evaluating clinical tool-using agents in synthetic emergency-medicine workflows, adapted from Corecraft. It combines an in-memory world, a Model Context Protocol tool surface, saved execution traces, and binary task criteria with an authored safety gate. The current inventory contains 205 tasks and 2,323 criteria. We distinguish historical benchmark measurements from a subsequent automated engineering programme focused on preserving evidence and exposing incomplete execution. Frozen V8 Pass@1 was 24.8% for Claude Opus 4.6 and 12.6% for GPT-5.4; these results used a grader subsequently found to fail open and are retained as superseded measurements, not estimates under the repaired implementation. The newer engineering work adds source-faithful projections, strict execution and grading boundaries, immutable attempts, and separately reported completion, physical storage, readback, and content verification. A source-bound local development cohort schedules 16 attempts across Nemotron Nano and MedGemma on 8 exposed synthetic reconciliation cases. Of these, 15 executions completed, 15 notes were physically stored, and 2 stored texts were retrieved again; none met the full mechanical source-reconciliation contract. Clinical criteria were unassessed. The associated engineering checkpoint records 4,588 passing tests and 58 skipped tests. This is a single-author physician-engineer project using automated development, verification, and local evaluation. It reports no independent clinical labels, measured operator benefit, product superiority, or deployment readiness. Optional offline inspection tools support debugging without introducing a human-review requirement into execution or publication. The release provides inspectable negative results and reproducible engineering evidence; prospective clinical validation and comparative user studies remain future research.
